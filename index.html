How Academic AI Similarity Detection Works

Why passages without obvious buzzwords can still be flagged as AI-generated

1. Sentence Predictability (Perplexity)

AI models choose high-probability verb chains ("plays a crucial role in shaping," "fosters a comprehensive understanding") and heavy abstract nouns ending in -tion, -ment, -ity. Human scholars use sharper, concrete verbs.

2. Uniform Rhythm (Low Burstiness)

LLMs typically write sentences that are uniformly 16–26 words long with flat declarative clauses. Human academic essays mix short 7-word punchy observations with longer 35-word evidence-heavy sentences.

3. Missing Empirical Anchors

Unedited AI paragraphs summarize concepts broadly without citing specific years, scholar names, dataset sizes (n = 412), or historical events. Adding concrete anchors immediately lowers AI similarity.
